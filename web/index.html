            BOUNDARIES ≠ PERMISSION

            The default source filters the Fee land records in the USGS Protected Areas Database of the United States (PAD-US) 3.0 for selected federal, state, and local public managers in California, Oregon, Washington, Idaho, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico, and Texas. It excludes private records and overlapping designation-only records. The live ArcGIS layer is hosted outside USGS and reproduces this older PAD-US release.

            PAD-US is a national inventory assembled from agency and partner data, not a real-time closure or camping-permission feed. Coverage and update dates vary by source and state. Its boundaries are generalized and may overlap or be incomplete.

            Do not use this map to decide whether a specific campsite is legal or open. Contact the managing office and check current restrictions, access, fire rules, and conditions. A missing boundary does not prove land is private or unavailable.

            Optional Forest Service and BLM recreation-site overlays, Forest Service roads, trails, MVUM routes, BLM road datasets, and alternate imagery/topographic basemaps are reference layers only. Forest Service dynamic map services render within a rectangular view and may include nearby adjacent-state areas at the region edges; PAD-US and BLM layers are filtered to California, Oregon, Washington, Idaho, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico, and Texas, and BLM recreation points are clipped to those state boundaries. Click any mapped Forest Service or BLM recreation-site symbol to see available site details; reported status and site type do not confirm current availability or permission to camp. Click a mapped USFS or BLM road to inspect its available route attributes, land record, coordinates, and distance to a named Census road. When the selected route matches connected Census road geometry, the popup gives a road-network distance; otherwise it says unavailable and keeps straight-line distance as a separate reference. Neither establishes camping permission or current road access/closures. Coverage and update timing vary. Verify with the responsible land manager.

            Offline maps

            Saved areas are snapshots from the day you download them. Offline, the map cannot show new closures, fire restrictions, or emergency orders, and place search and road-distance tools need a connection. Check current conditions with the land manager before you lose signal.

            The iPhone app can also save the OpenFreeMap street map. Under OpenFreeMap’s terms each download is limited to 3,000 tiles and 50 MB, so those areas must be smaller.
